Beginner●○○≈10 min

Meet the kit

Kick = the deep boom under your right foot. Snare = the sharp crack on backbeats. Hi-hat = the ticking "tss" that keeps time. Tap each pad below and match every sound to its name until it is automatic.

Try: play all nine pads with your eyes closed and name each sound.

Beginner●○○≈15 min

Your first rock beat

Count out loud: "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". Kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, hi-hat on every count. Load the Basic Rock preset in the Beat Lab, press play, and air-drum the pattern on your desk.

Try: loop the preset at 90 BPM and clap on 2 and 4.

Intermediate●●○≈15 min

Lock in steady time

A drummer's real job is keeping time. Set the tempo to 70 BPM and play hi-hat 8ths only — perfectly even. When that feels easy, layer the rock beat from the last lesson without rushing the kick.

Try: play along with the Beat Lab for two straight minutes without drifting.

Intermediate●●○≈15 min

Your first fill

A fill is a short break from the groove. On beat 4 of every fourth bar, play: snare – snare – tom 1 – crash. Land back on the kick exactly with the crash so the groove restarts clean.

Try: use the pads — Snare, Snare, Tom 1, then Crash on the next count of 1.

Advanced●●●≈20 min

Ghost notes & dynamics

Ghost notes are snare hits played at roughly 20% volume between the loud backbeats. Load the Funk Groove preset and imagine two snare volumes: accents you hear from the back row, ghosts you only feel.

Try: alternate one loud snare with three ghosts while the hi-hat stays unchanged.

Advanced●●●≈20 min

Linear grooves

Linear drumming means no two limbs strike together — the pattern flows in a single line. Build an 8-step line in the Beat Lab where kick, snare and hats never share the same step, then loop it slowly.

Try: a classic starter line — kick, hat, snare, hat, kick, hat, snare, hat.

Play the drum kit click the pads, or use keys 1–9

The 8 steps are 8th notes over one bar of 4/4. Step 1 = beat 1, step 3 = beat 2, and so on.

Piano

Beginner●○○≈10 min

Find your way around

Black keys sit in groups of 2 and 3 — every C lives just left of a group of 2. Find every C on the keyboard below and click it. Middle C (C4) is your home base for everything that follows.

Try: play all the C keys, then all the F keys, then all the G keys.

Beginner●○○≈15 min

The C major scale

Press "C scale" below and listen. Then play it yourself: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C with fingers 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5 — your thumb tucks under after the third note. An even tempo beats a fast tempo.

Try: play the scale up along with the demo, then back down without it.

Intermediate●●○≈15 min

Your first chords

Chords are three notes stacked: C (C-E-G), F (F-A-C), G (G-B-D). Hit the chord buttons below to hear each triad's colour, then build every chord on the keyboard yourself before you press its button.

Try: play C, then F, then G — holding each one for four slow beats.

Intermediate●●○≈20 min

The pop progression

C – G – Am – F is the skeleton of thousands of songs. Press "Play C–G–Am–F" to hear it, then play it back one chord per four beats, switching as smoothly as you can.

Try: play the progression twice through without stopping.

Advanced●●●≈20 min

Two hands, two jobs

Right hand plays the C scale; left hand holds one whole note per bar: C, then F, then G, then C. Slow everything to half speed — hands-together playing is a coordination skill, not a speed skill.

Try: four bars, hands together, twice in a row without a mistake.

Advanced●●●≈25 min

Inversions & the minor mood

Flip each triad so a different note is on top — C-E-G becomes E-G-C. Inversions let your hand slide between chords with tiny movements. Then learn the A-minor scale and hear how the mood darkens.

Try: play C, then its two inversions, then Am and its inversions.

Beginner●○○≈10 min

Meet your electric

Six strings, thickest at the top of the neck (low E) to thinnest at the bottom (high e). Tab numbers are frets: 0-2-2 means "open, 2nd fret, 2nd fret". Click the low E string below, then its 2nd and 3rd frets.

Try: play every open string from low E to high e and say its name out loud.

Beginner●○○≈15 min

Power chords: E5 & A5

Two fingers, endless rock. E5 = open low E plus the 2nd fret on A and D. Slide the same shape across two strings for A5. Press the E5 and A5 buttons below, then find both shapes on the fretboard.

Try: strum E5 four times, then A5 four times, in a slow steady rhythm.

Intermediate●●○≈15 min

Palm muting

Rest the side of your picking hand lightly on the strings right where they leave the bridge — instant chunky "chug". Alternate 4 palm-muted hits with 4 open strums of E5 and hear the two textures.

Try: palm-mute E5 on counts 1-2-3, open on 4. Repeat until it locks in.

Intermediate●●○≈20 min

The minor pentatonic

The rock solo alphabet: E-G-A-B-D on the low frets. Press "Show E minor pentatonic" below — the highlighted frets are position 1. Play it low string to high, alternate picking every note, then back down.

Try: up and down twice without looking at your picking hand.

Advanced●●●≈20 min

Build a riff

Riff recipe: E5 chugged ×8 → A5 ×4 → back to E5. Use rests — silence is part of the riff. Loop your two-bar idea until you can play it without watching your left hand.

Try: write a 2-bar riff with at least one rest and one accent.

Advanced●●●≈25 min

Bend, vibrato, burn

Bend the 7th fret of the B string up a whole step — check your pitch against the piano's D. Add vibrato by bending slightly and rhythmically. Finish with 16th-note pentatonic runs, metronome on.

Try: bend – hold – vibrato – release, four times, in tune.
